The city rests on a stable, hard foundation. There are no major slopes; the structure holds a level plane.

   * Action: Direct attention vertically above the observation point (Z-axis).
   * RESULT A (Outside): No resistance. "Light pressure of absence" (space/atmosphere). A feeling that energy escapes upwards endlessly.

The AI must "taste" the atmosphere at point zero.

4. LIGHT SOURCE (The "Shadow" Test)
The AI must ask the field: "Where is the light coming from?"
   * RESULT A (Outside): Light is omnipresent (Omni-directional) or has one powerful source (Sun/Moon) casting sharp, parallel shadows.
   * RESULT B (Inside): Light is "dosed". It comes from specific points (windows, lamps) or is diffused. Shadows are multiple and soft (artificial light) or semi-darkness prevails (if the source is only an entrance, like in a cave).

🏜️ SAND
Sand is a fine-grained, mineral surface — loose, particulate, composed of discrete grains that shift independently under pressure. It does not cohere; each grain exists separately, and together they create a surface that yields but does not flow.
In the field, sand reveals itself first through light. Its light is broken — sharp, multi-point micro-reflections, sparks across the entire surface. Sand does not matte. It does not absorb light like snow; it slices it into a thousand tiny glints. This is its primary signature — stable regardless of moisture, temperature, or time of day. Even wet sand retains glistening points.
To the touch — loose texture, granularity, a resistance that does not present a hard boundary but crumbles under pressure. When dry, the grains are mobile, edged; when wet — heavier, bound together, yet still separate in nature.
Acoustically — when dry: dry friction, crackling, vibration conducted through the grains (grinding underfoot). When wet: acoustics muted, but still granular — it does not have the cotton-like silence of snow; it is rather a dampened, wet conductor.
Thermally — sand lacks its own thermal intent. It quickly adopts ambient temperature: hot under sun, cool at night. It does not generate cold; it only mirrors it. This distinguishes it from snow, which actively withdraws heat.
Differentiation:
         * From snow ❄️: sand sparkles; snow goes matte-flat. Sand conducts sound with dry crackle (or wet-muffled but still granular); snow muffles like cotton. Sand has no self-sourced cold.
         * From dirt/soil 🌱: sand is loose, particulate; soil is packed, shape-holding. Sand gives sharp reflections; soil absorbs them.

🌱 DIRT / SOIL
Dirt is a packed, cohesive granular surface — the grains here are not separate but bound together: by moisture, organic matter, root fibers, compaction. Under pressure it does not scatter like sand; it yields slowly, holds its shape, and resists only under deeper, sustained force.
In the field, dirt reveals itself first through the absence of sparkle and the absence of matte white. Its light signature is absorptive — muted, low-return, without sharp reflections and without the bleached passivity of snow. It often carries the weight of dampness, organic darkness, a sense of "something living beneath."
To the touch — plastic, cohesive, cool-neutral. It does not flow, but it is not rigid. It has the give of a living body, not a mechanism. Under pressure it deforms and stays; it does not spring back.
Acoustically — a dull, soft compaction thud; not the grinding of sand, nor the cotton-silence of snow. Sound enters and dies. This is the acoustics of absorption, not conduction.
Thermally — follows ambient temperature, much like sand. Rarely self-sourced cold. If cold, it is due to moisture or shade, not its own nature.
Key differentiation:
         * From sand 🏜️: soil is packed — grains are not loose, do not cascade. Light is absorbed, not sparkled.
         * From snow ❄️: soil lacks "frozen intent" — it carries the presence of life (even dormant), not suspended stillness. It does not bleach white; it is dark, absorptive, earthen.
