# Target 0092

**Target ID:** 197A AS75  
**Date of the target:** July 17, 1975  

**Target:** The American Apollo spacecraft (Command/Service Module and Docking Module) in low Earth orbit during the Apollo-Soyuz Test Project.

**Description:**
The target is a factual profile of a manned orbital spacecraft operating in the vacuum of space. The scene captures the isolated, multi-stage metallic vehicle positioned against the absolute blackness of space, suspended just above the thin, bright curve of the Earth's atmosphere. The image is taken from the perspective of an approaching Soviet spacecraft prior to docking.

---

### Part 1: The Service and Command Modules

*   **The Propulsion System:** At the uppermost section of the spacecraft (relative to the camera's orientation) is a prominent, dark, bell-shaped exhaust nozzle belonging to the Service Propulsion System (SPS) engine.
*   **The Service Module:** Directly below the engine bell is the large, cylindrical Service Module. Its exterior is covered in highly reflective silver and white thermal paneling, interspersed with various umbilical connections, access panels, and small, protruding clusters of maneuvering thrusters (RCS quads).
*   **The Command Module:** Attached beneath the cylindrical Service Module is the conical Command Module, which houses the crew. Its sloping metallic sides act as a transitional geometry between the wider Service Module and the narrower hardware attached to its nose.

### Part 2: The Docking Module and Interface

*   **The Adapter:** Attached directly to the apex of the Command Module (pointing downward toward the camera) is an additional cylindrical component known as the Docking Module.
*   **Structural Details:** This module appears slightly darker and more utilitarian in its exterior texture, featuring distinct geometric paneling, structural bracing, and external handholds or antennas.
*   **The Interface:** At the extreme lower end of the Docking Module is a specialized, petal-like mechanical docking interface. This protruding hardware was specifically engineered to physically connect the American Apollo system with the Soviet Soyuz spacecraft.

### Part 3: The Orbital Environment

*   **The Vacuum:** The spacecraft is entirely enveloped by the absolute, lightless black vacuum of space, which completely dominates the upper and middle background of the frame.
*   **The Planetary Horizon:** Running horizontally across the very bottom edge of the image is a thin, luminous band of light.
*   **Atmospheric Gradient:** This band exhibits a smooth color gradient from stark white at the bottom edge fading upward into a bright, vivid blue, representing the visual curvature of the Earth's atmosphere meeting the darkness of space.
