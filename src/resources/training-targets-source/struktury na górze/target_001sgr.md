# Target 0088

**Target ID:** 744M PE15  
**Date of the target:** Present day   

**Target:** The ancient Inca citadel of Machu Picchu (originally Patallaqta), located in the Cordillera de Vilcabamba, Andes Mountains, Peru.

**Description:**
The target is a factual profile of a highly preserved, high-altitude archaeological site. The environment is characterized by steep, lush mountain peaks, extensive dry-stone agricultural terraces, and complex light-granite architecture seamlessly integrated into the rugged topography. The site reflects monumental urban planning, religious significance, and advanced structural engineering.

---

### Part 1: The Mountainous Topography and Environment

*   **The Location:** The citadel is situated on a high mountain saddle (elevation 1,999 to 2,400 meters above sea level) nestled between two prominent peaks: Machu Picchu ("old peak") and Huayna Picchu. The site is located 112 kilometers from Cuzco.
*   **The Slopes:** The sheer, almost vertical mountainsides surrounding the city plunge deeply into the valley below, where the Urubamba River flows.
*   **Agricultural Terraces:** The steep natural terrain is heavily modified by extensive agricultural terraces. These horizontal stepped platforms are 2 to 4 meters wide and are supported by vertical retaining walls constructed from dry-laid stone.

### Part 2: Urban Layout and Infrastructure

*   **The Masonry:** The city is constructed primarily from light-colored granite, utilizing advanced, mortarless masonry that perfectly conforms to the pre-existing rock formations to maximize the use of the challenging terrain.
*   **The Sectors:** The layout is divided into two main areas separated by a large, grassy central plaza. The upper, western sector (*hanman*) functioned as the cultural and ceremonial center, housing the royal palace and elite residences. The lower, eastern sector contained utilitarian production workshops and thatched-roof residential houses.
*   **Engineering:** Vertical movement across the terraced, multi-level city is facilitated by an extensive network of stone staircases, totaling approximately 1,200 individual steps. A sophisticated system of channels distributed water collected from rock-carved reservoirs.
*   **Measurement Systems:** Structural dimensions were strictly regulated using two standardized modules (*quanta*). A standard 42 cm cubit was used for utilitarian and servant (*yanaconas*) buildings. A 54 cm "royal cubit" was exclusively utilized for laying out the high-status residential and ceremonial structures.

### Part 3: Ceremonial Structures and Astronomical Alignment

*   **The Intihuatana:** Located at the highest point of the upper sector is the Intihuatana (the "place where the sun stops"). This is a large, monolithic stone pillar carved directly into the solid bedrock, functioning as the most sacred Inca object and a precise astronomical observatory.
*   **Solar Temples:** The ceremonial sector also features the Temple of the Three Windows and the Tower of the Sun, which is built on a horseshoe-shaped plan. These structures feature strategically placed window openings specifically aligned so that sunlight illuminates interior stones during the winter solstice.
