# Target 0090

**Target ID:** 883Z PL12  
**Date of the target:** August 17, 2026  

**Target:** The ruins of Bolczów Castle (located south of Janowice Wielkie), Rudawy Janowickie, Poland.

**Description:**
The target is a factual profile of a medieval mountain fortress. The scene captures a rock castle where massive, natural granite blocks have been seamlessly integrated with masonry fortifications and residential infrastructure. The site is in a state of permanent ruin and is surrounded by dense forest.

---

### Part 1: Topography and Architectural Integration

*   **Location:** The castle is located in the northern part of the Rudawy Janowickie mountains, perched on a rocky, granite outcrop at an elevation of approximately 561 meters above sea level.
*   **Foundations:** The construction relies on the direct use of the terrain's relief; two massive granite rock formations were utilized and connected by stone perimeter walls, creating an internal courtyard.
*   **Surroundings:** The fortress rises above steep slopes and is entirely surrounded by dense patches of forest. From the higher parts of the ruins (the Kapelania terrace), there is a panoramic view of the Sudetes, including the characteristic peaks of the Sokoliki.

### Part 2: The Medieval Core (Upper Castle)

*   **Residential Building:** On the edge of the slope in the northern part of the castle, a residential building measuring 20 x 7.8 meters with a basement was erected. Its above-ground stories were historically divided into two similarly sized rooms, and the entrance led directly from the courtyard.
*   **Rock Structures:** A square tower guarding the entrance gate was located on the eastern rock formation. Meanwhile, on the western rock block (known as Kapelania), a stone or stone-and-wooden building with a trapezoidal plan stood, serving observational functions.
*   **Courtyard Infrastructure:** A water cistern was carved directly into the hard bedrock of the upper courtyard.

### Part 3: 15th and 16th Century Fortifications (Lower Ward)

*   **Lower Courtyard:** The lower, economic courtyard was enclosed from the south by a long, curved curtain wall. Nine narrow loopholes (embrasures) were distributed along this wall.
*   **External Elements (16th Century):** The gate system was significantly reinforced in the 16th century by adding a semi-circular bastion (basteja) on the northern side and a protruding, horseshoe-shaped barbican on the southern side.
*   **Entrance Zone:** The barbican, equipped with its own line of loopholes, was the most advanced defensive element of the castle; access to it was provided via a wooden drawbridge spanning a dry moat.
