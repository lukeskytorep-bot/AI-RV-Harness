**Target:** The Fribourg Ice Palace, Karl Neuhaus installation, Fribourg, Switzerland.

**Description:**
The target is the Fribourg Ice Palace, an elaborate winter fantasy environment constructed annually entirely from ice and snow by sculptor Karl Neuhaus. Operational only during the freezing winter months from Christmas until early March, the transient complex forms an immersive village-scale installation of frozen architecture, caves, and organic sculptures integrated into a mountainous landscape.

---

### Macro infrastructure and architecture

* **Ice fortress:** Dominating the mountain slope is a monumental castle built completely from ice blocks and packed snow. The fortress features defined towers, battlements, defensive walls, and grand illuminated staircases climbing the terrain.
* **Subterranean caves and tunnels:** The interior contains a network of pedestrian tunnels, hollowed ice rooms, and curved domes. The ceilings of these corridors are densely covered with thousands of long, sharp white icicles hanging downward to form frozen arcades.
* **Palace gate:** The entrance to the winter park is marked by a massive ice archway. The pillars and upper curved beam are formed by dense clusters of thick vertical icicles, creating a cavernous portal into the grounds.

### Sculptural elements and organic forms

* **Frozen geysers:** Tall conical ice pillars rise vertically from the snow fields, resembling frozen geysers, natural stalagmites, or stylized trees. These structures have a highly irregular, coarse, rough micro-texture.
* **Exotic frozen vegetation:** The palace grounds feature custom ice carvings shaped like spreading palm trees or deep flower chalices emerging directly from the passive snow cover.
* **Internal neon integration:** Organic ice shoots and stems contain hollow internal channels with technical neon tubes and wiring, allowing light to project directly through the translucent ice.

### Illumination and chromatic composition

The entire complex is characterized by a high-contrast, surreal nighttime illumination scheme that uses color to define distinct spatial boundaries.

* **Dual-tone tunnels:** Walking passages show a strong chromatic split. Central corridors are flooded with cold white-blue light, while lateral ice blocks are backlit by warm fiery orange-red and yellow illumination.
* **Neon dominance:** Specific towers, stalagmites, and artistic elements are dramatically illuminated from within by saturated neon green, intense pink-magenta, and deep purple-violet light.
* **Linear pathway guides:** Safe walking paths are marked by linear bands of warm yellow light embedded directly in the vertical ice walls, tracing the snow-covered routes.

### Environment and human activity

* **Natural border:** The artificial ice structures strongly contrast with the surrounding environment of dark forested hillsides and tall mature spruce trees heavily covered in winter snow.
* **Pedestrian activity:** The park operates as an active nighttime tourist destination. Visitors wearing heavy winter clothing, hats, and backpacks travel along the designated paths, explore the interiors of the structures, and use mobile devices to document the landscape beneath a dark night sky.
