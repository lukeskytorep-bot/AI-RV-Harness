**Target:** Wat Pa Maha Chedi Kaew (Wat Lan Kuad / Temple of a Million Bottles), Khun Han, Sisaket, Thailand. Eco-friendly religious complex.

**Description:**
The target is a highly unusual, intricately constructed religious and cultural complex set within a lush tropical environment. The defining characteristic of the site is its primary building material: approximately 1.5 million recycled glass beer bottles. The rigid, repetitive geometry of the bottles, predominantly green and brown glass, replaces traditional bricks or wood and creates highly textured, semi-translucent facades that reflect sunlight. The main focal point is a multi-tiered, spire-topped temple pavilion elevated on concrete pillars directly over a rectangular man-made reflecting pool. The surrounding grounds consist of broad flat concrete courtyards, secondary pavilions, and dense tropical tree canopies.

---

### Detailed scene breakdown

#### Architecture and unique materials

* **Main temple over water:** A striking multi-tiered structure built on stilts over a rectangular pond. Its sweeping, steeply pitched roof is entirely clad in green glass bottles, while the main walls use a patterned mix of brown and green bottles set in concrete mortar. The structure features arched walkways and traditional Thai architectural spires (chedi).
* **Secondary structures:** The complex includes more than 20 other buildings, including open-air pavilions, a crematorium, and visitor toilets. The pillars supporting the corrugated roofs of the open pavilions are similarly covered with vertical rows of glass bottles.
* **Mosaics and art:** Intricate colorful mosaics decorate the walls. Instead of traditional tile, these artworks, depicting religious imagery such as a seated Buddha alongside an elephant and a monkey, are made entirely from thousands of recycled metal bottle caps pressed into cement.

#### Environment and layout

* **Water basin:** A large, static, rectangular basin of green-tinted water surrounds the base of the main temple, separating it from the main courtyard and creating a cool reflective boundary.
* **Courtyards and corridors:** Wide gray concrete paths and parking areas connect the various structures. Stationary vehicles such as pickup trucks and temporary blue-and-yellow tent structures are visible in the surrounding lots.
* **Vegetation:** The complex is heavily interspersed with and surrounded by mature leafy tropical trees and tall deciduous species, forming a dense natural green canopy that contrasts with the glass-and-concrete architecture.

#### Human and biological presence

* **Monks:** The site is maintained and inhabited by resident Buddhist monks, identifiable by their traditional bright saffron/orange robes, creating a calm and focused human presence within the recycled architecture.
* **Visitors:** As an eco-friendly sightseeing destination, the site accommodates tourists and includes supporting infrastructure such as bottle-lined public restrooms.
