**Target:** Silesian Railway Museum, Jaworzyna Śląska, Poland.

**Description:**
The target is a fact-based profile of an extensive historic railway and industrial hub that now operates as a living museum of technology. The scene shows a rugged post-industrial landscape dominated by monumental railway architecture, a dense network of tracks, and a large collection of heavy historic rolling stock.

---

### Part 1: Architecture and spatial layout

* **Site and scale:** The facility occupies more than 2.5 hectares of open space containing approximately 3 kilometers of railway track.
* **Fan-shaped roundhouse:** The main central architectural feature is a historic fan-shaped locomotive roundhouse built in 1906–1907. From the air, the building has a clearly semicircular form. On the inner side, it features a row of massive arched doors in blue and gray leading to 19 separate locomotive stalls.
* **Central infrastructure:** At the geometric center of the track layout is a large metal railway turntable. Tracks radiate from it directly toward the roundhouse stalls. A tall steel lighting tower rises above the turntable and adjacent yard. A gated railway crossing is located nearby.

### Part 2: Collections and heavy rolling stock

* **Collection scale:** The museum contains approximately 150 historic railway vehicles. The collection includes passenger and freight cars, specialist vehicles, and around 50 steam, diesel, and electric locomotives manufactured in countries including Poland, Germany, the United States, and England.
* **Outdoor display:** Massive black steam locomotives are displayed directly on the tracks in front of the roundhouse and along the main visitor routes, forming a dense steel landscape. Numerous freight and passenger cars are parked on sidings nearby, including distinctive vehicles with wooden construction.
* **Unique vehicles:** The museum's living-history mission includes maintaining operational machines such as TKt48-18, the only working steam locomotive in the collection, built in 1951; the Deutz OMZ 117R from 1940, described as the oldest operational diesel locomotive in Poland; and the unique Warszawa M20 rail inspection vehicle.

### Part 3: Interiors and technical facilities

* **Workshop hall, interior:** The interior of the roundhouse is a rugged, shadowed post-industrial space whose roof is supported by massive multi-branched wooden trusses.
* **Historic artifacts:** Preserved and reconstructed spaces display historic locomotives and wooden railway cars. Authentic old station signs, including “JAWORZYNA ŚL.” and “Nach Friedland-Halbstadt,” and other historic slogans hang above them.
* **Technical facilities:** The museum maintains a fully functional historic railway workshop used for ongoing maintenance of rolling stock. Historic technical infrastructure includes a Henschel railway drop pit from 1930, an ash-cleaning channel, water cranes, and a coal crane. The interiors also contain seven separate themed exhibitions, including the ODRA Workshop with a period computer, the Railway Ticket Office, and the station master's offices.
