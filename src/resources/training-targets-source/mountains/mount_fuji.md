**Target:** Mount Fuji (Fuji-san), Honshu, Japan.

**Description:**
The target is a fact-based profile of Japan's highest peak and an active stratovolcano. The scene shows a monumental, isolated mountain with a highly symmetrical form, surrounded by lakes, forests, and traditional cultural architecture, and serving as a destination for mass pilgrimage and tourism.

---

### Part 1: Volcano and topography

* **Structure:** The target is a massive stratovolcano rising to 3,776 m above sea level. It was formed from three overlapping volcanic cones resting above a deep magma chamber.
* **Cone and summit:** The mountain has a highly symmetrical conical shape. Its broad upper sections are covered by a white layer of snow and ice, sharply contrasting with the dark, snow-free lower slopes. Viewed from orbit, the volcano casts a large triangular shadow across the surrounding landscape and clouds.
* **Crater:** At the summit is a crater approximately 500 meters in diameter and 250 meters deep. Its inner and outer rims are rugged and formed from bare, sharp volcanic rock completely devoid of vegetation.

### Part 2: Infrastructure and human presence

* **Trails and mountain huts:** Zigzagging hiking paths cross the rugged volcanic slopes, with simple flat-roofed huts positioned along them and partly built into the rock.
* **Tourists:** Dense groups of climbers and pilgrims move along the marked trails, equipped with hiking gear and heavy backpacks.
* **Summit and architecture:** A stone obelisk with Japanese inscriptions and a meteorological observatory station stand on the crater rim. At lower elevations and near the foot of the mountain, traditional Japanese architecture is visible, including characteristic multi-level pagodas with red roofs and white details.

### Part 3: Surroundings and land use

* **Water and forests:** On the northern side, the volcano is surrounded by the Fuji Five Lakes system (including Lake Shōji), whose calm dark-blue waters form a foreground for the mountain rising behind them. Dense dark-green forests extend around the foot of the mountain, including the well-known Aokigahara forest.
* **Geographic setting:** Mount Fuji dominates relatively flat surrounding terrain and lies at the junction of three tectonic plates. From a satellite perspective, the immediate volcanic slopes are free from dense urbanization, which is concentrated in valleys and around the lakes.
* **Secondary features:** East of the volcano lies a large Japan Ground Self-Defense Force training area covering 88 km², as well as the vast Fuji Reien cemetery covering 2.3 million m², with major avenues lined by cherry trees (sakura).
