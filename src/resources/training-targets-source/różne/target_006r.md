# Target 0098

**Target ID:** 294V OK02  
**Date of the target:** 18.08.2026  

**Target:** The "Kuroshio Sea" main tank at the Okinawa Churaumi Aquarium, Ocean Expo Park, Okinawa, Japan.

**Description:**
The target is a factual profile of an immense, engineered aquatic enclosure designed to simulate a deep ocean environment. The scene captures a massive volume of captive saltwater contained behind a monumental transparent barrier, populated by gigantic pelagic fish, and observed by crowds of human spectators from a darkened gallery.

---

### Part 1: The Enclosure and The Barrier

*   **The Tank:** The central environment is a gigantic, artificially constructed saltwater basin (measuring 35 meters long, 27 meters wide, and 10 meters deep). It holds approximately 7,500 cubic meters (7,500,000 liters) of continuously pumped seawater.
*   **The Acrylic Window:** The primary interface between the aquatic environment and the human observers is a colossal, seamless transparent panel. To withstand the immense hydrostatic pressure without shattering, it is constructed of acrylic plastic rather than glass, measuring 8.2 meters in height, 22.5 meters in width, and 60 centimeters in thickness.
*   **Lighting and Depth:** The interior of the tank is brightly illuminated, glowing with a deep, vibrant blue gradient that simulates the open pelagic zone of the Kuroshio current. 

### Part 2: The Pelagic Marine Life

*   **The Apex Subjects:** Dominating the aquatic space are massive whale sharks (some reaching up to 8.5 meters in length). These enormous, spotted filter-feeders move slowly and deliberately through the upper and middle water columns.
*   **The Manta Rays:** Gliding fluidly alongside the sharks are multiple captive-bred manta rays, including giant oceanic mantas, reef mantas, and a rare all-black variant. They exhibit a smooth, "flying" kinetic motion through the water.
*   **The Biomass:** Filling the remainder of the artificial sea are thousands of smaller schooling fish, swimming in synchronized, swirling patterns around the larger predators.

### Part 3: The Human Observation Gallery

*   **The Viewing Area:** Immediately outside the massive acrylic barrier is a multi-level, dry observation gallery. 
*   **The Spectators:** The gallery is intentionally darkened, filled with the stark, black silhouettes of hundreds of human visitors. They stand tightly packed at the base of the window, pointing, observing, and taking photographs.
*   **The Contrast:** There is a profound spatial and atmospheric contrast between the vast, bright, weightless blue world of the marine creatures and the dark, gravity-bound, confined architectural space occupied by the human observers.

### Part 4: Additional Surrounding Facilities and Exhibits

*   **The Ocean Expo Park & Dolphin Stadium:** The aquarium is situated within a larger coastal park. A short distance from the main building is the Okichan Theater, an open-air stadium and lagoon facility featuring active, leaping dolphins and human trainers.
*   **Other Marine Tanks:** The immediate vicinity includes several distinct aquatic environments: a roofless "Coral Sea" tank exposed to direct natural sunlight, a "Shark Research Lab" containing aggressive predators (bull and tiger sharks), and a highly pressurized, darkened "Deep Sea" exhibit holding rare, deep-water organisms.
*   **Auxiliary Pools & Structures:** The exterior grounds feature dedicated, open-air pools for manatees and sea turtles (including artificial sandy spawning beaches), as well as a traditional Okinawan house constructed of stone and wood with a tiled roof.
*   **Research Operations:** The location functions as an active scientific research center. Remote viewers may perceive advanced laboratory equipment and biological interventions, such as the use of artificial uteruses for shark embryos and medical treatments for large marine mammals.
